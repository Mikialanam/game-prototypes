# General Games Report: What Makes Casual Games Win, and Which Idea to Build First

*Prepared November 2026 for Miki. Audience: adults and teens, not kids. Every claim links to its source. Figures from analytics firms (Sensor Tower, AppMagic and others) are estimates, not audited numbers.*

---

## Summary

- **Hits are simple, quick to learn, and easy to share.** Flappy Bird, Wordle and 2048 were small games built fast. They spread because players had something to show friends. Copying a hit almost never works: clones flooded the stores within weeks, and the stores pushed back.
- **Habit comes from a short loop plus a reason to return tomorrow.** Daily puzzles, streaks and comparing scores with friends do the most work. Near misses ("so close!") make players retry, but the evidence is mixed and the tactic needs care.
- **Money.** Only about 2% of players ever buy anything. Rewarded ads (watch an ad, get a reward) suit short-session games. Subscriptions work for brain and puzzle products that feel useful (NYT Games, Elevate). Cash prizes bring real legal risk and should wait.
- **Market.** Merge and tycoon games earn the most money but need big teams and big ad budgets. Tower drop is crowded and runs on ads alone. Math sprint has a real, underserved niche: people preparing for trading, consulting and finance tests currently use a bare-bones website.
- **Recommendation: build Math Ring first,** as a daily, shareable math sprint aimed at finance and consulting minded adults. The smallest worthwhile test is one daily 60-second sprint that is the same for everyone, with the ring extension, one rewarded-ad continue in practice mode, a streak, and a shareable result card. Do not claim it makes you smarter. The FTC fined Lumosity for that.

## 1. Why hit casual games succeeded, and why copies failed

### The hits

**Flappy Bird (2013–14).** Made in about the length of a game jam (a few days). Its brutal difficulty made every death feel like the player's own fault, so they retried at once ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2023/04/flappy-bird-rise-fall-viral-mobile-gaming-phenomenon)). The creator said it earned about $50,000 a day from ads before he pulled it ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/john-brandon/flappy-bird-syndrome-why-kill-a-50000-per-day-app.html)). He later said he removed it because it had become too addictive ([Quartz](https://qz.com/175499/why-the-wild-success-of-flappy-bird-apparently-burned-its-creator)).

**Wordle (2021–22).** One puzzle a day, the same for everyone, was a deliberate choice and one of its smartest features ([Northeastern University](https://news.northeastern.edu/2022/01/21/what-makes-wordle-so-g-r-e-a-t/)). The emoji share grid let players brag without spoiling the answer, and the game took off after that feature arrived ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/josh-wardle-reflects-on-the-the-unconventional-road-to-wordle-s-success)).

**Candy Crush Saga (2012–13).** It was free to play. When you ran out of five lives, you waited 30 minutes per life or paid 99 cents ([Bangor Daily News/AP](https://www.bangordailynews.com/2013/10/08/business/candy-crush-maker-gets-players-to-pay-up-for-free-game/)). Facebook friends could send each other lives, and a map of levels showed progress ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/candy-crush-saga-reaches-new-heights-as-designer-explains-its-success/)).

**Angry Birds (2009).** Rovio had made 51 other games before it ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/stories/51-failed-games-rovio-created-angry-birds-now-going-public-1-billion-valuation/)). It passed 500 million downloads in under two years ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/rovios-smash-hit-angry-birds-surpasses-half-a-billion-downloads)).

**2048 (2014).** A free web riff on the paid game *Threes*. It spread faster than the original, and the Threes team called the copies "rip-offs" after spending over a year on their design ([Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/news/game-clones-threes-developer-slams-rip-copycats)).

### Why similar games failed

- **Clone floods.** About 15 new Threes/2048 clones reached the App Store every day at the peak ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/threes/there-are-15-new-threes-2048-clones-released-on-the-app-store-every-day)). Apple and Google started rejecting games with "Flappy" in the name that tried to pass as the original ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2014/02/17/flappy-bird-clones-rejected/)). Copies split the attention and few lasted.
- **Being first and free beat being better.** Threes was arguably the better-designed game, but the free 2048 won the audience ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/058583/r/)).
- **Volume of attempts.** Rovio's 51 earlier games show that most attempts miss, even for good teams ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/stories/51-failed-games-rovio-created-angry-birds-now-going-public-1-billion-valuation/)). Many big-studio games are also cancelled in soft launch, a small test release in a few countries ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/soft-launch-trends/)).

### Patterns to take away

1. **Learn it in five seconds.** One tap, one rule.
2. **Failure feels fair and fast to retry.**
3. **A built-in reason to share**, ideally without spoilers.
4. **A ritual.** Once a day beats unlimited play for word of mouth.
5. **Cheap to make, many tries.** Small teams win by testing many ideas quickly.
6. **The idea itself is easy to copy.** What lasts is the community, the daily habit and the brand.

## 2. What makes a game engaging and habit-forming

### Core loop
Flappy Bird rounds lasted seconds and restarted instantly, which kept players in a tight play, fail, retry loop ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/flappy-bird-and-the-eight-secrets-to-optimal-gameplay-25603)). Math Ring's sprint, ring attempt and retry fits this well.

### Difficulty curve
Flow theory says players stay engaged when the challenge matches their skill: too easy is boring, too hard causes anxiety ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-flow-applied-to-game-design)). A 2016 study found that steady difficulty was *not* best. Short spikes of overload followed by easier stretches produced more flow and enjoyment ([Baumann et al., Motivation and Emotion](https://www.uni-trier.de/fileadmin/fb1/prof/PSY/PGA/unterlagen/BaumannL%C3%BCrigEngeser_Flow_MOEM_2016.pdf)). For Math Ring: ramp problems up, then give a breather problem now and then.

### Near misses
In a Candy Crush lab study, near misses produced the strongest urge to keep playing, and also the most frustration ([Larche et al., Journal of Gambling Studies](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10899-016-9633-7)). A racing-game experiment did not find the same boost ([Finserås et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11469-019-00070-9)). Takeaway: showing "you were 2 points from your best" is fair and useful. Faking near misses is manipulative and the evidence for it is weak.

### Streaks
Duolingo calls the streak one of its biggest growth levers ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). Its share of daily users who come back the next day hit a record 84% ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/duolingo-daily-active-users-reach-58-7-million-as-retention-hits-record-84-and-social-accounts-top-1-billion-organic-impressions/)). A streak "freeze" for missed days reduces the anger of losing one (same Lenny's source).

### Daily challenges
Wordle's single daily puzzle created a shared ritual people could talk about ([Northeastern](https://news.northeastern.edu/2022/01/21/what-makes-wordle-so-g-r-e-a-t/)). NYT Games built a subscription business on daily puzzles and an archive of past ones ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/north-america/new-york-times-games-puzzles-wordle-subscriptions/)).

### Social features
Meta's game platform recommends friend leaderboards as its top retention tool, because every name on the list is someone you know ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/games/retain/leaderboards)). Published hard numbers are scarce, though, and most claims come from vendors (same source). A share card (like Wordle's grid) is the cheapest social feature to build.

### Retention targets to aim for
Typical mobile games keep about 25–30% of players on day 1, 10–15% on day 7, and 3–6% on day 30. "Good" is about 35–40%, 18–22% and 8–10% ([AppAgent](https://appagent.com/?p=61637)). Puzzle games run near 32% / 12% / 5% ([Nudge](https://nudgenow.com/blogs/mobile-game-retention-benchmarks-industry)).

## 3. Monetization

### What the numbers say
- Only about 1.8–1.9% of mobile players buy anything in a given period ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/just-1-9-percent-of-mobile-players-make-a-purchase-says-new-report)). Just 0.23% of players produced 60% of in-game revenue in one study ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/in-mobile-games-60-of-revenues-come-from-0-23-of-players/)).
- **Rewarded ads:** one studio reported 75–80% of players chose to watch ads ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/next-games-ads-drive-revenue/)). Rewarded video made up about 39% of casual-game ad revenue in early 2025, per a TopOn report ([Prado](https://prado.co/post/rewarded-video-holds-the-crown-but-interstitial-ads-are-catching-up)).
- **Hybrid is the trend.** Pure ad-funded "hyper-casual" games still get the most installs (27%) but only 11% of play sessions, because players churn fast ([Adjust via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-mobile-games-insights-report)). Studios are adding purchases and deeper progression to simple games ([Unity](https://unity.com/en/blog/the-shift-from-hyper-to-hybrid-games-and-why-it-matters-for-everyone)).
- **Subscriptions** work when the product feels useful. Elevate charges about $39.99 a year in the US ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id875063456)). Sensor Tower estimated its US revenue at about $302K a week in late December 2025 ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q4-unified-top-5-trivia-games-revenue-us-604118ed241bc16eb8b8453a)).

### Fit for each idea

| Idea | Best fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Math Ring | Rewarded ad continue + optional subscription (no ads, drill packs, stats) | The "extend" moment is a natural ad slot; test-prep users pay for useful tools |
| Tower drop | Ads only | Classic hyper-casual; low revenue per player |
| Train switcher | Ads + level packs | Puzzle levels can be sold or unlocked |
| Merge a city | In-app purchases (energy, boosters) | Genre earns through purchases and live events |
| Property tycoon | In-app purchases + rewarded ads | Idle and board-game tycoons rely on both |

**Design warning for Math Ring:** if a daily leaderboard lets players watch ads for extra time, scores stop being fair. Keep ad continues in practice mode, or show the "no-help" daily score separately.

### Cash prizes and prize pools: legal basics (not legal advice)

- **The three-part test.** A game with a **prize**, **chance**, and **consideration** (paying to enter) is generally an illegal lottery. Remove any one and you may be fine ([Olshan Frome Wolosky](https://olshanlaw.com/sweepstakes-law-basics)).
- **Free sweepstakes** keep prize and chance but must offer a truly free way to enter. A purchase cannot improve your odds ([RTM](https://www.rtm.com/blog/no-purchase-necessary-laws-and-your-sweepstakes)).
- **Paid skill contests** (entry fee, winner by skill) depend on state law. Most states ask whether skill is the *dominant* factor. Stricter states ban any meaningful chance, or treat the entry fee itself as a wager ([Ifrah Law](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/ifrah-on-igaming/gambling-on-skill-enforcement-of-gambling-laws-against-skill-games-in-outlier-states/); [FirstAmendment.com](https://www.firstamendment.com/list-states-skill-gaming-allowed-prohibited/)).
- **App store rules.** Apple requires the developer to sponsor any contest, show official rules in the app, and state that Apple is not a sponsor ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/660501); [Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Real-money games fall under stricter gambling rules, and one developer with a free prize app was asked for gambling licenses ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/825845)). Google Play has its own real-money policy that varies by country ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12918670)).
- **Trust risk.** Skillz won a $42.9M patent verdict against rival AviaGames, and the case surfaced claims that AviaGames used undisclosed bots against paying players ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/jury-awards-skillz-42-9m-in-patent-infringement-trial/); [Law.com](https://law.com/litigationdaily/2024/02/16/litigators-of-the-week-in-mobile-gaming-showdown-a-side-battle-over-bots)).
- **Teens:** the audience includes teens, which makes cash prizes harder still. Get a lawyer's view before any money changes hands.

**Bottom line:** skip cash prizes for the first version. If you want prizes later, start with a free-entry contest funded by you, with a lawyer reviewing the rules.

## 4. The market for each idea

### Big picture
Puzzle games made about $14.4B in mobile revenue in 2025, second only to strategy, per a summary of Sensor Tower's annual report ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026)).

### Math Ring (math sprint)
- **Brain-training apps:** Elevate is the strongest, at roughly $150K–$300K a week in US revenue during 2025 ([Sensor Tower Q2](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q2-unified-top-5-trivia%20games-revenue-us-604118ed241bc16eb8b8453a); [Sensor Tower Q4](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q4-unified-top-5-trivia-games-revenue-us-604118ed241bc16eb8b8453a)). Peak earns far less, at a few thousand dollars a week in Europe ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q2-unified-top-5-trivia%20games-revenue-europe-604118ed241bc16eb8b8453a)).
- **Trading and finance interview prep:** Zetamac, a free two-minute arithmetic website, is the community default ([TechInterview](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474557/mental-math-trading-interviews/)). Firms like Jane Street, Optiver, SIG and Citadel Securities reportedly use timed mental-math screens (same source). Optiver's "80 in 8" (80 questions in 8 minutes, wrong answers penalized) is the best-known test, though its current use is uncertain ([QuantVault](https://quantvault.org/optiver-80-in-8.html)). Flow Traders' test leans on fractions, decimals and percentages ([QuantVault](https://quantvault.org/mental-math-for-trading-interviews.html)).
- **Exams:** GMAT volume fell about 19% to 93,196 exams in the year to June 2025 ([Poets&Quants](https://poetsandquants.com/2025/10/29/global-gmat-test-volume-fell-19-in-2025-gmac-confirms/)). Exam prep is a real but shrinking hook. Don't make it the main pitch.
- **The "AI age" angle:** a Microsoft and Carnegie Mellon survey of 319 knowledge workers found that people who trusted AI more did less critical thinking ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/02/11/microsoft_study_ai_critical_thinking/)). It is a self-reported survey, so use it for marketing tone, not as proof.
- **Claims risk:** Lumosity paid $2M to settle FTC charges that its brain-health claims were unsupported ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges)). Say "practice mental math", not "boost your IQ" or "prevent decline".
- **Gap:** no polished mobile product combines Zetamac-style speed drills with a Wordle-style daily ritual, share card and streak, and finance-flavored problems (percentages, fractions, quick estimates).

### Tower drop
Ketchapp's *Stack* (2016) is the reference title ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/stack/id1080487957)). It runs on ads, and users complain about an ad after every round ([Marlvel](https://marlvel.ai/intel-report/games/com-ketchapp-stack)). Hobby developers keep releasing clones ([itch.io](https://brightcode.itch.io/wall-stack)). **Gap:** small. This is the declining hyper-casual model.

### Train switcher
*Train Conductor World* is the main example: switch colored trains onto matching tracks, rated 4.7 from about 4.8K US ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id932228293)). That rating count suggests a niche audience, not a mass hit. **Gap:** a daily train-routing puzzle could work, but demand is unproven.

### Merge a city
A huge but tough genre. *Gossip Harbor* was estimated at about $35M a month, ahead of *Travel Town* at about $21M ([Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/?p=16793)), with $677M lifetime revenue per AppMagic ([Udonis](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/gossip-harbor)). Smaller titles such as *Merge Mayor* are estimated near $200K a month ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/paywall-library/merge-mayor/)). **Gap:** none for a solo developer. These games need a lot of art, story, live events and ad spending.

### Property tycoon
*Monopoly Go* passed an estimated $6B in purchase revenue in under three years ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/monopoly-go-app-revenue-milestone)), built on a famous brand and heavy live operations. Smaller tycoons like *Business Empire: RichMan* drew tens of thousands of weekly US downloads in 2024 ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2024-q3-unified-top-5-business%20games-units-us-6023dfa3241bc16eb8392c45)). *Idle Miner Tycoon* earned roughly $25K–$33K a week on US Android ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2024-q1-android-top-5-miner%20games-revenue-us-6023e454241bc16eb89ccd46)). **Gap:** a finance-literate tycoon (real cash flow, cap rates, debt) could set itself apart, but it is a large build.

### Lights Out puzzle (existing prototype)
This fits the "daily puzzle" pattern and the brain positioning. It could later become a second daily game inside the same app, the way NYT Games bundles several puzzles ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/north-america/new-york-times-games-puzzles-wordle-subscriptions/)).

## 5. Recommendation

### Pursue Math Ring first

**Why:**
1. **A clear niche with a weak incumbent.** Serious users rely on Zetamac, a plain website ([TechInterview](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474557/mental-math-trading-interviews/)).
2. **It matches the hit patterns:** one-minute loop, fair failure, a daily ritual, shareable scores.
3. **A natural ad moment.** The "extend" step fits a rewarded ad, which players accept readily ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/next-games-ads-drive-revenue/)).
4. **Subscription upside.** People pay for tools that feel useful, as Elevate shows ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q4-unified-top-5-trivia-games-revenue-us-604118ed241bc16eb8b8453a)).
5. **Cheap to build and change.** The prototypes already exist.

**Positioning:** "The daily 60-second math sprint for people who work with numbers." Lead with finance, consulting and trading. Mention exams second. Keep claims modest (the Lumosity lesson).

### Smallest version worth testing

Build it as a web page first, as Wordle did, so links can be shared without an install:

1. **Daily Sprint:** 60 seconds, the same seeded problems for everyone each day. Problems mix arithmetic, percentages, fractions and quick estimates.
2. **Ring extend:** when time runs out, one ring-tap attempt to win bonus seconds.
3. **Share card:** score, accuracy, streak and a small emoji grid, with no answers shown.
4. **Streak** with one free "freeze" per week.
5. **Practice mode:** unlimited sprints, plus one rewarded-ad continue (a fake ad on web is fine for the test). The daily score stays ad-free so it's fair.
6. **Analytics:** day-1 and day-7 return rate, share rate, practice runs per day, continue opt-in rate.

**Leave out for now:** accounts, global leaderboards, cash prizes, the subscription, other game modes.

**Pass/fail targets (2–4 weeks, a few hundred players from finance and consulting communities):**
- Day-1 return ≥ 35% and day-7 ≥ 15%, the "good" band ([AppAgent](https://appagent.com/?p=61637))
- At least 1 in 10 daily players shares a result
- Over half of eligible practice players accept the ad continue

If it passes, wrap it as a mobile app and test the subscription. If it fails on returns but not shares, work on difficulty and variety. If nobody shares, rethink the share card or the audience.

### Order for the other ideas
1. Lights Out as a second daily puzzle in the same app.
2. Train switcher as a possible third daily puzzle.
3. Tower drop only as a quick ad-funded experiment.
4. Merge city and property tycoon: park until there is a team and an ad budget.

## Open questions for Miki

1. **Who exactly is the first user?** Trading-firm applicants, consulting candidates, working finance people, or GMAT/GRE takers? Each points to different problem types and channels.
2. **How important is money in year one** versus proving demand? This decides whether the subscription test comes early.
3. **Web first or app first?** Web makes sharing easier; an app makes streak reminders easier.
4. **Should the ring step stay a reflex test,** or become a math moment (for example, an estimate for bonus time) so the brand stays "pure brain"?
5. **Are cash prizes a must-have long term?** If yes, budget for a gaming lawyer before building anything that pays out, especially with teens in the audience.
6. **Do you have access to finance or consulting communities** (alumni groups, LinkedIn, campus clubs) to recruit the first few hundred testers?
7. **One app with several daily puzzles** (Math Ring, Lights Out, trains), or separate apps?
